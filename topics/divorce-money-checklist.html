the grown-up stuff · legal

the divorce money checklist

the emotional part gets all the attention. the financial part is where the lasting damage happens — usually through paperwork nobody explained. here's the order.

9 min readupdated august 2026topic: legal

the short version

  • retirement accounts are often the largest asset in a marriage, and they're the most commonly mishandled.
  • a 401(k) or pension requires a QDRO — a separate court order — to split. an IRA doesn't; it uses a different process called a transfer incident to divorce.
  • done right, a QDRO transfer triggers no taxes and no 10% penalty. done wrong — by just withdrawing and handing over cash — it triggers both.
  • you need a separate order for each plan. one QDRO does not cover a 401(k) and a pension.
  • gather documents before you file. access to shared accounts and records tends to get harder, not easier, once proceedings start.

gather before you file

The single most useful thing you can do early is boring: collect paper. Access to joint accounts, shared logins, and household records gets harder once proceedings begin, and reconstructing them later costs lawyer hours you'd rather spend elsewhere.

What to pull copies of:

  • Tax returns — three to five years, with all schedules.
  • Retirement statements — for every account, both names. Courts look closely at when contributions were made, so collect statements showing balances before the marriage, during it, and after separation.
  • Pay stubs and income records for both of you, including bonuses and equity.
  • Bank and brokerage statements — 12+ months.
  • Debt statements — cards, loans, mortgage, and your own credit reports from all three bureaus.
  • Property and insurance documents — deeds, titles, policies.
  • Any prenup or postnup. These may protect accounts, but only if valid and enforceable.

two rules while this is happening

Don't move or drain marital assets. After a divorce starts, disposing of marital assets is generally not permitted — and emptying an account doesn't stop your spouse from claiming their marital share anyway, so you can end up paying twice plus penalties. Keep your behavior stable and documented from the moment proceedings begin.

marital vs. separate — and why the state matters

Broadly, what you brought in is separate and what you built together is marital — but the line is state-specific and the details do the work.

In community property states, income and assets accumulated during marriage are typically marital regardless of whose name is on the account. Equitable distribution states divide based on fairness rather than a straight split — which can mean more or less than half.

The most common misunderstanding: your name on the account doesn't make it yours. A 401(k) entirely in one spouse's name, funded entirely by that spouse's paycheck, is still generally marital property to the extent it grew during the marriage.

Worked example: if one spouse had $50,000 in a 401(k) before marrying and it grew to $150,000, the $100,000 of growth during the marriage is generally what's on the table — not the whole balance.

Marriage length also matters — shorter marriages often produce smaller shares, longer ones more equal division.

the QDRO, explained

A Qualified Domestic Relations Order is a court order telling a retirement plan administrator to pay part of one spouse's benefits to the other (called the "alternate payee").

It exists because a divorce decree alone isn't enough. Employer plans are governed by federal law, and the plan administrator needs this specific order to transfer funds without tax penalties.

account typewhat's needed
401(k), 403(b), pension, TSPa QDRO — one per plan
IRA (traditional or Roth)no QDRO — a "transfer incident to divorce"

You need a separate order for each employer plan — a 401(k) and a pension are two orders, not one. And the order must spell out the exact dollar amount or percentage.

the mistake that costs the most

Some people skip the QDRO and just withdraw from the 401(k) to pay the ex directly. That converts a tax-free transfer into a taxable distribution plus a 10% early-withdrawal penalty if under 59½ — and the account holder eats both. Done properly via QDRO, the transfer avoids early-withdrawal penalties entirely if the receiving spouse rolls it directly into an IRA.

if you're the one receiving

You have a choice most people don't know exists, and it's genuinely valuable if you need cash.

Option one — direct rollover to your own IRA. The standard move. Preserves tax deferral, no taxes now, and the new IRA is treated as your own for future required minimum distributions.

Option two — take some as cash. Here's the part worth knowing: if you need money immediately, a lump-sum distribution taken under the QDRO is subject to income tax but not the 10% early-withdrawal penalty, even if you're under 59½. That penalty exception is unique to QDRO distributions.

The catch: it generally has to come directly from the plan under the QDRO. Roll it into an IRA first and then withdraw, and you've lost the exception. If you need cash from your share, decide before the transfer, not after.

the trade nobody frames properly

You may be offered a swap — you keep the house, they keep the retirement account. Equal-sounding numbers aren't equal assets. A house carries maintenance, taxes, insurance, and a mortgage; a retirement account grows and requires nothing. And "equal value" often ignores that pre-tax retirement dollars will be taxed on withdrawal while home equity may not be. Get both valued on an after-tax basis before agreeing.

the after-divorce list

The decree isn't the end. These are the things that quietly go wrong in the year after:

  1. Update every beneficiary designation. Your decree does not remove your ex from your 401(k) — only the form does. This is the single most-skipped step, and it has cost people entire accounts.
  2. Confirm the QDRO was actually executed. Approved isn't transferred. Follow up with the plan administrator until the money has moved.
  3. Separate everything. Accounts, cards, insurance, phone plan, utilities. Joint debt remains joint in the eyes of the lender regardless of what the decree says.
  4. Pull your credit reports. Check for joint accounts you forgot and any debt opened in your name.
  5. Update your will, power of attorney, and healthcare directives.
  6. Count the years of the marriage. If it lasted 10+, you may be eligible to claim Social Security on your ex's record later — worth up to 50% of their benefit, without their knowledge or consent.

the bottom line

Divorce is where the accumulated financial decisions of a marriage get unwound under time pressure, often by people who weren't the ones tracking them.

You don't need to become an expert. You need to know that retirement accounts require a specific order, that the order is separate from the decree, and that the paperwork after the decree matters as much as the decree itself.

Your next tiny step: start a folder — physical or cloud, somewhere only you can reach — and put the last three years of tax returns in it. Everything else builds from having the documents.

questions people actually ask

do i need a qdro for an ira?

No. IRAs aren't ERISA plans, so a divorce decree or property settlement instructing the custodian to transfer ownership is generally sufficient — a process called a transfer incident to divorce.

Employer plans like 401(k)s and pensions are the ones that require a QDRO.

who pays for the qdro?

It varies and is negotiable — sometimes split, sometimes paid by the spouse receiving the funds. Costs depend on plan complexity and whether a QDRO specialist drafts it.

Many divorce attorneys outsource QDRO drafting to specialists, so ask early whether it's included in your fee.

can i keep my whole 401(k)?

Sometimes — if you offer other marital assets of comparable value, like home equity, and your spouse agrees.

Just make sure the comparison accounts for taxes and carrying costs. Pre-tax retirement dollars and home equity are not the same dollars.

what if my ex hides assets?

Hiding assets in divorce is not permitted, and discovery exists to surface them. Forensic accountants specialize in this when the numbers don't add up.

This is exactly why gathering tax returns and statements early matters — the paper trail is your leverage.

how long does a qdro take?

It varies. After a judge signs and the plan administrator approves, transfers into another retirement account may occur within a few weeks.

Delays usually come from drafting errors and plan rejections, which is why specialist drafting is worth it.
