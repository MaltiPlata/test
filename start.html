something is ending

a marriage, a relationship, a life you shared.

i'm getting divorcedwhat to gather, what to open, and what to check before you sign anything.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    copy every statement — bank, retirement, mortgage, debts, and the last 3 years of tax returns. copies are enough.

  2. 2

    open an account in your name only and pull your free credit report, so you know exactly what's in your name.

  3. 3

    list what you own and owe, and check every beneficiary before you sign anything.

i need to leave — quietlysafety first, then your documents, then money that's only yours.

if someone can see this device, use a private window. the quick exit button (bottom right) leaves instantly.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    safety first. call 800-799-7233 or text START to 88788 — an advocate can build a safety plan with you, free and confidential.

  2. 2

    quietly copy your documents — IDs, birth certificates, account info — and keep them where only you can reach them.

  3. 3

    start a small amount of money that's only yours, even $20 at a time, somewhere he can't see.

i'm divorcing after 50retirement money and social security matter more than the house.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    get every retirement account on paper — 401(k)s, IRAs, pensions. these are often the biggest assets, not the house.

  2. 2

    if you were married 10+ years, you may be able to claim social security on your ex's record. check before you agree to anything.

  3. 3

    ask for a QDRO (the court order that splits a 401(k) or pension) so your share moves without taxes or penalties.

my partner diedthe first weeks, what you're entitled to, and what can wait.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    breathe — most decisions can wait. don't sell, move, or sign anything big for a few months unless you have to.

  2. 2

    order several certified death certificates; banks, insurers, and agencies each want one.

  3. 3

    call social security about survivor benefits, and check every account's beneficiary — those pass outside the will.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    negotiate the first offer — every raise after is built on it.

i'm starting my first jobthe W-4, the paycheck, and the account to open first.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    fill out your W-4 carefully — it decides whether you owe or get a refund in april.

  2. 2

    grab the 401(k) match if there is one. it's part of your pay.

  3. 3

    open your own savings account and send a little from every check there automatically.

i'm signing my first leasewhat you're agreeing to, and how to get your deposit back.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    read the whole lease — fees, renewal, who fixes what. ask before you sign, not after.

  2. 2

    photograph everything on move-in day and email the photos to yourself and the landlord.

  3. 3

    get renters insurance — it's usually cheap, and it covers your stuff, not just theirs.

taking care of people

kids on your own, a parent who needs you.

i'm raising kids on my ownthe filing status, the credits, and the paperwork that protects them.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    file as head of household and claim the child tax credit — up to $2,200 per child.

  2. 2

    name a guardian in a will and check every beneficiary, so a court doesn't decide for you.

  3. 3

    build your cushion — aim for more than the usual 3 months when you're the only income.

i'm caring for a parentthe paperwork to do while they can still sign it.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    get a durable power of attorney and a health care proxy signed while your parent can still make decisions.

  2. 2

    make a list of their accounts, bills, and insurance — where they are and how to log in.

  3. 3

    protect your own money: keep contributing to your retirement, and track what you spend on their care.

i got a job offerbefore you say yes — say thank you, then ask.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    don't accept on the spot. say thank you and ask for the full offer in writing.

  2. 2

    look up what the role pays where you live, and pick your number before the call.

  3. 3

    ask for more — base pay first, then benefits, title, or flexibility.

i came into moneyan inheritance, a settlement, a bonus.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    park it somewhere safe and boring — a high-yield savings account — and wait 30 days before deciding anything.

  2. 2

    pay off high-interest debt first. that interest outruns almost any investment.

  3. 3

    then decide, with help if it's big — a fee-only advisor who isn't selling you anything.

i got a surprise bill or ticketpaying right away isn't always the move.

your first three steps

  1. 1

    ask for an itemized bill — errors are common, especially on medical bills.

  2. 2

    ask for a payment plan or a reduction before it goes to collections. many places say yes.

  3. 3

    for a ticket, read your options — traffic school, a reduction, or contesting it.
