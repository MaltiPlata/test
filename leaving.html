when you need options

the quiet checklist.

this is for the woman who isn't sure yet, or who is sure and needs a plan. getting your paperwork and money in order isn't a decision to leave — it's just having your own footing. do as much or as little as fits your situation.

if you're in danger, start here
  • National Domestic Violence Hotline — call 800-799-7233, text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org. free, confidential, 24/7, 200+ languages.
  • StrongHearts Native Helpline — 844-762-8483, for Native American and Alaska Native communities.
  • if you're in immediate danger and it's safe to do so, call 911.
  • advocates can help you build a safety plan — that's a specific thing they do, and it's free.

the paperwork, in order.

the goal is simple: copies of what proves who you are, and access to money that's yours. take it one item at a time.

1

copy the documents first

photograph or scan them and store them somewhere only you can reach — an email account he doesn't know about, a cloud folder, or with someone you trust. copies are enough to start; you don't need originals.

  • driver's license, passport, social security card, birth certificate
  • the same for each of your children
  • marriage certificate, any custody or court orders
  • last two years of tax returns, recent pay stubs
  • lease or mortgage, car title, insurance policies
  • bank, retirement, and credit card statements — even joint ones
2

open an account in your name only

at a different bank than any shared account, so it doesn't appear in the same online login. use a mailing address that isn't home — a PO box, a work address, or a trusted friend's. ask the bank for paperless statements so nothing arrives in the mail.

3

know the numbers before anything changes

write down what comes in, what goes out, and what's owed — including debts in your name and any joint debt you'd still be responsible for. pull your free credit report at annualcreditreport.com; it will show accounts you may not know exist.

4

build the cushion quietly

even small amounts add up, and having any money of your own changes what's possible. cash back at checkout, a side deposit, a gift from family — whatever fits without drawing attention. keep it somewhere separate from daily spending.

5

secure your digital life

change passwords on a device he can't access, and turn on two-factor where you can. check location sharing on your phone and in family plans, shared calendars, and car apps. a shared cloud account can sync photos, messages, and location without anyone touching your phone.

6

get one free legal conversation

you don't need money for this. legal aid offices, domestic violence advocates, and many bar associations offer free consultations. ask specifically what your state says about leaving the home, custody, and joint accounts — those three answers vary the most and matter the soonest.

one honest note: laws about property, custody, and joint accounts vary a lot by state, and the order you do things in can matter legally. this is a starting map, not legal advice — a local advocate or legal aid attorney can tell you what applies where you live, for free.
