then. now. next.

it is genuinely recent that a woman could sign for her own money. if you've ever wondered why this stuff feels like it was never explained to you — here's the actual reason, with dates.

in 1974, a woman could not get a credit card, a mortgage, or a business loan in her own name. Before the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, banks required a male cosigner — a husband, father, brother, or male friend — who was assumed to be more creditworthy even if he was in jail. Lenders could and did cancel a woman's card when she got divorced, and asked wives about their birth control, sometimes requiring a doctor's letter before approving a loan.

then

the milestones that had to be legislated, because they weren't given.

  1. 📜 1862

    The Homestead Act lets unmarried women claim land in their own name — one of the first federal openings for American women to hold significant property independently.

  2. 🖋️ 1974

    The Equal Credit Opportunity Act. Signed October 28, 1974, it made it illegal to discriminate against a credit applicant on the basis of sex or marital status. Women could finally apply for a credit card, a mortgage, or a loan without a man's signature.

    Drafted largely by Emily Card, who couldn't get credit herself while working full time; introduced with Rep. Bella Abzug, and written into law by Rep. Margaret Heckler. In 1974 there were sixteen women in the House and none in the Senate. · National Archives

  3. 🏦 1975

    The First Women's Bank opens in New York City — owned and run by women, founded with Betty Friedan among its backers. Regulation B, which implements the ECOA, takes effect the same year.

  4. 🧾 1976

    The ECOA is amended to prohibit discrimination on race, color, religion, national origin, age, and receipt of public assistance — and to require lenders to explain why an application was denied.

  5. 💼 1988

    The Women's Business Ownership Act. Fourteen years after the ECOA, women could finally get business loans without a male relative cosigning. Women-owned businesses surged afterward.

    Forbes

  6. 👶 1993

    The Family and Medical Leave Act gives eligible workers up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave for family and medical reasons — still unpaid, still the federal baseline.

Do the math on that last one: every woman on any "most powerful women" list today was born into a country where she could not have opened a bank account without a man's permission. That's not ancient history. That's your mother.

now

the gap is smaller and it is not closed.

81¢ paid to women per dollar paid to men, full-time year-round — and it widened two years running, the first back-to-back widening since the 1960s.
~70% of the $124 trillion transferring by 2048 is expected to go to women — the largest shift of wealth by gender in history.
+0.4% how much women's investment returns beat men's annually, across 5.2 million accounts. the confidence gap was never a competence gap.

next

what's actually still open — and where you come in.

  1. 📈 the ownership shift

    Women are projected to control roughly two-thirds of US private wealth by 2030. Receiving money and having authority over it are different things — the next gap is about who makes the decisions, not who holds the account.

  2. 🛠️ building, not just earning

    It took until 1988 for women to borrow for a business without a man. Now the barrier to starting one is closer to zero — an LLC is a form and a fee, a shop is an afternoon, and software can be built by describing it. The gap left is knowing that.

    Financial abuse appears in 99% of domestic violence cases and 78% of Americans don't recognize it as abuse. Pay stays unspoken. The cheapest, fastest lever left is simply talking about money in public.
